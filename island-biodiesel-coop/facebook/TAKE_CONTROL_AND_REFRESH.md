# Facebook: take control of and refresh the existing account

The co-op already has a Facebook presence. This guide makes sure **the co-op, not any one person, controls it**, and then brings it in line with the new website and brand.

Work through it in order. Tick items off as you go.

---

## Step 1 — Find out what you have

Facebook has two kinds of account, and the right next step depends on which one this is.

| What you see | It's a… | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| "Add friend" button, a friends list, someone logs in *as* the co-op | **Personal profile** | Meta's rules say profiles are for individual people. A business run as a profile can be suspended without warning, can't run ads, has no insights, and is tied to one login. **Move to a Page (Step 2).** |
| "Like"/"Follow" button, a category under the name (e.g. "Energy company"), admins manage it from their own accounts | **Page** | Good. Skip to Step 3. |

- [ ] Account type: ☐ Profile ☐ Page
- [ ] Current URL: `facebook.com/__________`
- [ ] Who currently logs in to / manages it: ______________________
- [ ] Is the login email one the co-op controls (not a former volunteer's personal address)? ☐ Yes ☐ No

## Step 2 — If it's a personal profile: move to a Page

Meta has offered a tool to convert a profile into a Page, carrying friends over as followers. Check whether it's available for the account (search Facebook Help for "convert profile to Page"). If it is:

1. **Download a copy of the profile's information first** (Settings → Your information → Download your information). Conversion moves the profile picture, cover and followers, but not everything else carries over.
2. Run the conversion while logged in as the profile.
3. Immediately add at least two board members as admins (Step 3), then stop logging in to the old account.

If the conversion tool isn't offered:

1. Create a new Page from a board member's personal account (Pages → Create new Page), name **Island Biodiesel Co-op**, category **Energy Company** or **Cooperative**.
2. Post on the old profile for 2–4 weeks: "We've moved! Follow our official Page at …" and pin it.
3. Change the old profile's name/bio to point to the Page, then deactivate it once followers have moved.

## Step 3 — Lock in co-op ownership of the Page

The goal: no single volunteer leaving, losing their phone, or falling out with the board can lock the co-op out.

- [ ] **Create a Meta Business portfolio** (business.facebook.com, formerly "Business Manager") in the co-op's legal name, using a co-op email address such as `media@[YOUR-DOMAIN]`.
- [ ] **Claim the Page into that portfolio** so the co-op, as an organisation, owns it.
- [ ] **Two or more full-control admins**, both current board members or the staff media lead. Never just one.
- [ ] Give everyone else **task access** only (create content, reply to messages, view insights) — not full control.
- [ ] **Remove** anyone who no longer volunteers or works with the co-op.
- [ ] Every admin turns on **two-factor authentication** on their personal Facebook account (an authenticator app, not SMS, where possible).
- [ ] Record all of this in `../media-kit/ACCOUNTS_REGISTER.csv` (who has which role — **no passwords** in that file).
- [ ] Put a review of Page access on the board agenda **every quarter**.
