Our fuel

Biodiesel is a renewable diesel replacement made from fats and oils. Ours is made from used cooking oil collected right here on the island.

What is biodiesel?

Biodiesel is made by reacting vegetable oil or animal fat with an alcohol (usually methanol) and a catalyst. The process — transesterification — splits the oil into biodiesel and glycerin. The result runs in diesel engines and can be used on its own (B100) or blended with petroleum diesel.

Because it's made from waste oil rather than fossil fuel, biodiesel from used cooking oil has one of the lowest lifecycle carbon footprints of any liquid fuel.

Using biodiesel well

Will it work in my engine?

Most diesel engines run on biodiesel blends. Check your owner's manual or manufacturer for the highest blend they approve. Older engines (roughly pre-1993) may have natural-rubber hoses and seals that biodiesel can degrade over time; these are inexpensive to replace with modern materials.

Why change my fuel filter after switching?

Biodiesel is a mild solvent and loosens deposits left in your tank by petroleum diesel. Those deposits collect in the filter, so plan to change it after the first few tanks.

What about cold weather?

Biodiesel thickens ("gels") at higher temperatures than petroleum diesel. In cold spells, use a lower blend such as B20 and store equipment sheltered where possible. Ask us what blend we recommend this season.

Is it OK for boats?

Yes — and it's especially welcome on the water: biodiesel is biodegradable and far less toxic than petroleum diesel if spilled. Keep tanks topped up to limit condensation, since biodiesel attracts water more readily.

How long can I store it?

Use within about six months for best results. Keep it in a sealed, opaque container out of direct sun and heat.
